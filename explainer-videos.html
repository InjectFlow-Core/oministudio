How to make an explainer video with AI that looks and sounds professional

Last updated 13 October 2026.

You no longer need a camera, editing software or a designer to make a good explainer video. You need something worth explaining and a voice to explain it. AI can handle the drawing and the editing. What you say, how it sounds and what you ask for are still up to you, and that is what this guide covers.

Every explainer, however it is made, goes through the same five steps:

  1. 1IdeaOne question to answer
  2. 2VoiceRecord it, or turn a script into speech
  3. 3BriefWho it is for, and the tone
  4. 4ReviewWatch the draft, ask for changes
  5. 5PostTitle, caption, the right length

1. Pick one question to answer

The explainers people finish answer one question that someone actually asks. “What happens when I make a phone call?” works. “Everything about phones” does not. If you can’t say what your video answers in one sentence, it is trying to do too much.

  • Keep it to one question per video. A minute or two is often enough.
  • If the topic is big, split it into a series. Three short videos get watched more than one long one.
  • Use the question as your title. It is what people type into search.

2. Write it the way you’d say it

People listen to an explainer, they don’t read it. Write short sentences with one idea each, use everyday words, and read the whole script aloud before you record it. Wherever you stumble, your viewer will lose the thread too.

Leave web addresses, long numbers and codes out of what you say. They are hard to follow by ear and easy to show on screen instead. Our guide to writing voice-overs that sound natural covers numbers, names, pauses and pronunciation in detail.

3. Decide who narrates

You have three choices, and each one suits a different kind of video.

ChoiceBest when
Your own recordingPeople know you, or your personality is part of the video. Teachers, coaches and founders usually sound most convincing as themselves.
An AI voiceYou would rather not record, you need a different accent or tone, or you are making many videos quickly from written scripts.
A copy of your voiceYou want to sound like you without recording every video. You record a short sample once, then type scripts.

If you record yourself, the room matters more than the microphone. A phone held close in a quiet room with curtains, a sofa or clothes around it sounds better than an expensive microphone in an empty kitchen. Hard, bare walls bounce your voice back and make it sound distant.

Bare, echoey room

A few more habits that make a recording sound professional:

  • Keep the same distance from the microphone all the way through.
  • Switch off fans, music and notifications, and close the window.
  • Explain it the way you would to a friend across the table. Don’t perform.
  • Record a ten-second test and listen back on headphones before doing the whole thing.

If you use an AI voice, try one sentence with a name or a number in it before you generate the whole script. It is the quickest way to hear whether the voice and the pace suit your subject.

4. Tell it who the video is for

The same explanation looks different for a ten-year-old, a parent or an engineer. One sentence about your viewer and the tone you want does more than a long brief. Say who is watching, how it should feel, and anything you especially want shown.

Too vagueClear
Make it engaging.For curious teenagers and their parents. Friendly and simple, no technical terms.
Professional style.For new staff on their first day. Calm and reassuring, like a colleague showing them around.
Explain the product.For small shop owners who have never used online payments. Show the money moving from the customer to the shop.

If something must appear exactly as written, like your website, your product’s name or a slogan, say so separately. Don’t leave it for the AI to guess the spelling.

5. Review the draft like you’re giving notes to an editor

The first draft is a starting point. How good the finished video is depends on how you review it.

  • Watch it all the way through once without stopping, the way a viewer would. Note where you got bored or lost.
  • Then watch it with the sound off. The pictures should still roughly tell the story.
  • Ask for one change at a time, so you can see what each one did.

Good notes say where, what and how. “Make it better” gives an editor, human or AI, nothing to work with.

Vague noteUseful note
This part is confusing.When I say the call travels to the tower, show the signal moving from my phone to the tower, then on to theirs.
Too much going on.At the start, show only the phone. Bring in the tower when I mention it.
Wrong word.The website should read oministudio.com, all lowercase.

6. Get it ready to post

A good video can still get lost with a weak title or the wrong shape. Before you post:

  • Pick the right shape. Tall (9:16) for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp status. Wide (16:9) for YouTube, websites and presentations.
  • Check the length. Each platform has a limit, and shorter usually does better.
  • Write the title as the question. “How does a phone call work?” beats “Video 3”.
  • Write a caption for each platform. A YouTube description and a TikTok caption are read differently.
  • Watch it once on your phone. That is where most people will see it.
