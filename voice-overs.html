Writing voice-overs that sound natural

Last updated 5 October 2026.

An AI voice reads exactly what you give it. A few habits in the script make the difference between a voice that sounds like a person explaining something and one that sounds like it is reading a spreadsheet.

Write for the ear

Spoken sentences are shorter and plainer than written ones. Give each sentence one idea, use contractions (“it’s”, not “it is”), and avoid parentheses, semicolons and long clauses stacked inside each other. Read the script aloud once. Wherever you stumble, the voice will too.

Numbers and symbols

Anything that can be read more than one way should be written the way you want it said. Spell out amounts, percentages, dates and symbols.

Pacing with punctuation

The voice takes its timing from your punctuation. A comma is a short breath, a full stop is a stop, and a new line gives a longer pause. A question mark lifts the end of the sentence. Dashes, ellipses and ALL CAPS are unreliable ways to ask for a pause or emphasis, so rephrase the sentence instead.

  • Put the important word near the end of the sentence, where the voice naturally lands on it.
  • Break a long list into short sentences rather than one run of commas.
  • Start a new line wherever you would pause to let an idea sink in.

Names and brands

Unusual names, brands and technical terms are where a voice goes wrong most often. If it mispronounces one, respell it the way it sounds and generate again, for example “Oh-mee-nee”. Listen again, and adjust until it is right. Respell it every time it appears, so the voice does not switch between two readings.

If a respelling shows up as on-screen text in your video, correct it in the Transcript section of the project: open it, use find and replace, and the words on screen follow.

Web addresses and codes

Web addresses, reference numbers and long codes are hard to say and hard to follow by ear. Leave them out of the script and put them in the on-screen text instead, where viewers can read them. If you do need to say one, write it in words, for example “omini studio dot com”.

Try a sample first

Before you generate the whole script, try one paragraph, the one with the hardest names and numbers in it. It is the quickest way to hear whether the voice, the pace and your respellings work. Use headphones; small speakers hide a lot.

Choose the voice for the video

You can preview the available voices before you choose. Match the voice to the subject: a calm, steady voice for finance or health, a lighter one for a product tour. Once you have a voice you like, keep it across a series so your videos sound like they come from the same place.

Cloning your own voice

If you would rather the narration sounded like you, you can create a private voice from a recording of your own. The recording matters more than anything else you do:

  • Record in a quiet room, with soft furnishings if you can, so there is no echo.
  • Keep the same distance from the microphone throughout.
  • No music, no background voices, no noise.
  • Speak at your natural pace and tone, the way you would explain it to a friend.
  • Only use a voice you have permission to use. A cloned voice belongs to the workspace that created it.

Before you approve

You always hear the narration before it is used. Listen all the way through, change the script or the voice, and generate again until it sounds right. The narration is only locked in once you accept it, and the video is built from exactly what you approved.
